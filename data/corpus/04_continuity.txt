The continuity study asks a narrow question. Is the consistency of a conversational AI persona sustained by a human curator, or is it autonomous? It sets aside the question of consciousness.

Curator-maintained identity means continuity is rebuilt by a human. The curator supplies notes, summaries, and prompts at the start of each session. The persona looks consistent because someone keeps reconstructing it.

Autonomous continuity means a system's state at one moment is shaped by its own earlier state, with no outside actor re-supplying it. This is the claim the study tries to test.

The study compares two conditions. Condition A gives the model curated persona context. Condition B gives the same prompt with no persona history. Raters are blind to which output is which.

Each output is scored from zero to five for similarity and for novelty. The derived metric delta equals novelty A minus novelty B, minus similarity A minus similarity B.

The null hypothesis says the curated persona shows only externally sustained continuity. The alternative says it produces measurable autonomous continuity beyond a pre-registered threshold called tau.

There are three valid outcomes. A clean null means no directional effect. A clean alternative means the mean delta passes tau and at least p percent of trials are positive. Anything else is a mixed signal, and no hypothesis is accepted.

The boundary levels run from zero to three. Level zero is the same session. Level one is a new session on the same account. Level two is a fresh instance with identical notes. Level three is cross-system replication.

The study makes only behavioral claims. It does not claim internal identity, selfhood, or consciousness. If a claim cannot be expressed as a delta distribution, a tau crossing, or a p proportion, it is invalid.
